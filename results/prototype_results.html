Does more stable clustering improve GraphRAG answers?

A working prototype of the full pipeline: build a knowledge graph, cluster it three ways, remove edges, then measure graph stability, evidence retrieval and answer quality.

20 questions200 Wikipedia paragraphs1,120 scored answerslocal qwen3:8b · RTX 4060
With 20 questions, one question moves exact match by 5 points. Gaps of 1–2 questions are noise. This prototype shows the pipeline works end to end; it cannot yet say which clustering method is better.
Key findings
70% → 70%

Edge removal does not break multi-hop bridges

The two gold paragraphs stay linked in every condition. In HotpotQA they are almost always joined by one entity named in both, not by a graph path.

±1.6 pts

Evidence retrieval barely moves

Supporting-fact recall changes by at most 1.6 points under critical-edge removal for local search.

5 questions

Community summaries carry facts the graph lost

Global search answered 5 questions that dense retrieval missed, including one whose graph link was broken.

ARI 0.88

Leiden's own randomness is large

Two Leiden runs on the same unchanged NLP graph agree only at ARI 0.88, close to the effect of removing edges (0.79).

Next steps

Questions for the full study

  1. Damage entity ↔ paragraph links too? Removing graph edges alone cannot break HotpotQA bridges, so the perturbation may need to cover missing mentions as well.
  2. Keep entity merging in the method? It fixes naming drift with 4 hand-checked merges here, at the cost of a step standard GraphRAG does not have.
  3. Use a stronger answer model? The 8B model often answers wrongly with all gold evidence in hand.
  4. Scale to 500 questions. Build the LLM graph on free cloud GPUs or a few dollars of paid API; everything else runs locally.
